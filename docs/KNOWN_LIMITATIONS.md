# Known limitations
- iOS cannot send silently or in the background; every message needs a user tap.
- Android direct send is blocked from Play distribution until the SMS permission policy is resolved.
- Delivery reports depend on the carrier; absence does not mean failure.
- SIM subscription ids are device-local; phone numbers of SIMs are not read.
- Segment counts are estimates, not carrier billing.
- The iOS Swift bridges have never been compiled or run on a device. The Android SMS bridge has been run on one Samsung phone.
- Android composer fallback (system messaging handoff when direct sending is disabled) is not implemented yet.
- If the process dies while Android callbacks are pending, those messages become `unknown` even if the carrier sent them.
- Campaigns created before schema v2 have no saved audience: edit them and pick recipients again before scheduling or resending to current members.
- Android background sending needs Alarms & reminders permission; without it, scheduled runs only start while the app is open. Samsung and other vendors may still delay or block wake-ups unless battery use is Unrestricted / the app is a "Never sleeping app".
- The background service waits 2 minutes after the last message for delivery reports; reports arriving later are not recorded (the message stays `sent`). Messages still `submitted` when it stops become `unknown` the next time sending starts.
- Google Play: the special-use foreground service and exact alarms need Play Console declarations before a Play release.
- On iOS a scheduled run is only prepared; you must open it and tap Send for each message.
- Send limits count messages, not SMS parts (a 2-part message counts once), and only Android direct sends from this app; messages sent from other apps on the same SIM aren't counted.
- Send limits don't apply on iPhone, where every message is sent by hand.
- Messages-app fallback: Android doesn't report whether you tapped Send in the Messages app, so those messages are recorded as sent on your confirmation. They aren't counted towards the send limits and get no delivery reports.
- "Refused for good" detection relies on Android's rationale flag after the app has asked at least once; some phones (and permissions turned off from system settings) may show the normal Allow button first, which then does nothing — use App setup › Open app settings.
- Picture and group messages (MMS) are not downloaded (Part C, deferred). While this is the default messaging app, an incoming MMS only shows a notice; switch back to the previous messaging app to read it. Play may ask for MMS support during review (docs/play/permission_declarations.md).
- Contacts sync is read-only: edits to phone contacts are made in the phone's Contacts app. On iOS every sync reads all contacts (no "changed since"), notes are not read, and changes are only picked up when the app opens or returns to the foreground.
- If several phone contacts share a number, an incoming text is matched to one of them.
- Email: no delivery or open tracking; "Sent" means the email service accepted it. Replies (e.g. "STOP") arrive in your From mailbox, not in the app; mark those people opted out yourself.
- Email: the API key is stored in the app's private storage (not the encrypted keystore).
- Gmail needs a Google Cloud project and client ids in the build (docs/GMAIL_SETUP.md); until Google verifies the app, only up to 100 test users can connect. In the background Google may require signing in again: the run then waits for you.
- Email limits pause a campaign until they allow more; on iPhone, a limit-paused email run resumes when you open the campaign and tap Send.
- Mixed text+email campaigns: an SMS limit or email limit pauses the whole campaign until it resumes.
- Pro is checked on the phone only (no server). A rooted or modified phone could fake it; acceptable at this price, and the billing code can move to a server or RevenueCat later without changing the screens.
- On Google Play the app learns a subscription ended when it next asks Play (app start or return); offline, the last confirmed Pro status lasts up to 7 days.
- On the free app the signature is pre-filled in the Messages-app and iPhone composer; the user can delete it there before sending.
- App Store subscriptions are written but untested until the iOS app is built and signed.
- "20 at once" (Messages-app fallback): whether it goes as separate texts or a group MMS is decided by the user's Messages app and its settings; the app can't control or detect it, and records the batch as sent on the user's confirmation. iPhone still sends one at a time.
